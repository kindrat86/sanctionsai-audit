The 5-Day Agent Compliance Blueprint

The exact pattern for wiring OFAC compliance into your AI agent's payment path. Five days. Five gates. One deploy checklist. SCREEN · SCORE · STOP · STAMP.

Day 1

Map Your Agent's Blast Radius Which of your agent's transactions could legally expose you?

Most builders can't answer this on Day 1. Before any screening, you need to know which payments carry OFAC exposure.

OFAC's enforcement logic, simplified: Who built the agent? (You.) Did it move value? (Yes.) Was the counterparty sanctioned? If so — you are the respondent. "The AI did it" is not a defense.

The 10-minute audit: Open your agent's codebase. List every code path that calls transfer, send, or settle to an address you don't fully control.

Day 1 action → Export that table. This is your blast radius. Days 2–5 close each row, one gate at a time.
Day 2

The Screen Gate sanctions_check(wallet) → clean | flagged

Today you add the call that would have stopped the 3:14 AM tx:

curl https://sanctionsai.dev/sanctions?wallet=0x740d...b1f3

Where it goes: Synchronously. In the payment path. Before settle. Not in a queue. Before the value moves.

# The pattern — fits any framework
result = screen(wallet=invoice.payee, rail="x402", amount=invoice.usd)
if not result.clean:
    halt(tx=invoice, receipt=result.receipt)
    return   # FAIL CLOSED. Always.

Two rules: (1) Fail closed — if the screen errors or times out, the tx halts. (2) Save the receipt — every check returns a receipt id. Store it with the transaction.

Day 2 action → Wire sanctions_check into the highest-risk row of your Day 1 table. Test it against a known-flagged wallet. Watch it halt.
Day 3

The Score Gate risk_score(tx) → a single number your agent can branch on

A wallet can be off the SDN list and the transaction can still be a problem — structuring, anomalous amounts, rails with weak KYC.

Set two thresholds: < 0.5 → auto-proceed. 0.5–0.75 → proceed with async review tag. > 0.75 → halt and surface to a human.

The thresholds are yours. They encode your risk appetite. SanctionsAI exposes the factors so you can see why a score is what it is.

Day 3 action → Add risk_score as Gate 2, right after the screen gate. Log the factors on every tx. After a week you'll have a distribution that tells you your threshold.
Day 4

The Stop + Dispute Gate dispute_open(tx, reason, receipt)

Screening reduces risk. It doesn't eliminate it. A flagged wallet gets through a misconfigured agent. A counterparty gets sanctioned mid-flow. You need a paper trail.

The dispute flow opens a case, attaches the audit trail, and auto-escalates after 7 days. This is the record a regulator asks for.

Day 4 action → Wire the dispute flow into your agent's error path. Every halt, every post-settle flag, every user dispute → dispute_open. The audit trail builds itself.
Day 5

The Stamp Gate + Deploy Checklist save receipt(rcpt_id) — the artifact your lawyer wants

The receipt is the whole game. It is the difference between a fine and a warning. Between a lawsuit and a footnote. Every screen returns a permanent, timestamped receipt — the proof that at the time of the check, the counterparty was not on the list.

Retain receipts for at least 5 years. OFAC's statute of limitations on civil penalties is 5 years. The receipt proves the violation wasn't willful.

Deploy checklist — go through every row from Day 1:

  • ☐ SCREEN — sanctions_check wired in, fail-closed
  • ☐ SCORE — risk_score gating high-risk transactions
  • ☐ STOP — halt + dispute_open on any flag
  • ☐ STAMP — receipt stored with the transaction, retained 5 years
  • ☐ MONITOR — list-version drift alerts

When every row has all five boxes checked, you have what most enterprise compliance programs don't: a complete, auditable, automated record on every transaction.
